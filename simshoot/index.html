Abstract

TL;DR SimShoot reformulates aesthetic portrait reshooting as interactive photographic action planning with a video diffusion action simulator—turning precise edit-extent control into visual frame selection along a continuous simulated transition.

Aesthetic portrait reshooting aims to improve portrait aesthetics through adjustments available during a photoshoot, including camera control, pose, expression, and lighting. Existing image editors can produce appealing outputs without faithfully realizing requested photographic changes, while their single-image outputs make execution extent difficult to control.

We propose SimShoot, which couples a vision-language planner and selector with a video diffusion model adapted as a photographic action simulator. For each proposed adjustment, the simulator generates a gradual transition from the current portrait, exposing outcomes at different execution extents as the action unfolds across successive video frames. The selector chooses a suitable frame, and the selected portrait and visual feedback guide subsequent planning.

We construct single-action training videos using rendered camera trajectories (BlenderSynth), endpoint-conditioned portrait generation (RealSynth), and a separate linear-light synthesis route for relighting. In both open-ended and targeted reshooting, SimShoot surpasses the evaluated reshooting and open-weight editing baselines on portrait-specific aesthetic metrics. It achieves aesthetic performance comparable to the proprietary Nano Banana 2.1 in open-ended reshooting and surpasses it across all evaluated aesthetic metrics in targeted reshooting. SimShoot also achieves the strongest instruction following among all compared methods.

Method Overview

Instead of editing a portrait in a single opaque pass, SimShoot separates which photographic action to take from how far to execute it—coordinating three modules across an iterative closed loop.

VLM Planner

Proposes one structured DSL photographic action \(a_t\) at a time (closed-set Camera & Lighting, or open-vocabulary Pose & Expression) conditioned on initial portrait \(I_0\), current state \(I_t\), optional goal \(u\), and cross-round feedback.

VDM Action Simulator

A frozen MiniMax H3 video backbone paired with 7 single-action LoRA adapters (\(r = 16, \alpha = 16\)) rolls out \(c(a_t)\) from \(I_t\) as a continuous \(T = 39\)-frame transition \(V_t = (I_{t,0}, \dots, I_{t,T-1})\).

Coarse-to-Fine Selector

Inspects 5 evenly spaced frames \(\{F_0, F_{10}, F_{19}, F_{29}, F_{38}\}\) starting with unchanged \(I_t\), and optionally refines promising local intervals at zero extra video cost to choose \(I_{t+1}\)—or rejects and retains \(I_t\).

Ablation 1 · Selector (Action-Extent Selection)

Coarse-to-Fine Frame Selection vs. Terminal Frame Only

What we ablate: SimShoot's VLM frame-selection mechanism that controls how far a simulated action should go. Given the exact same 39-frame trajectory, we compare committing blindly to the final frame (w/o Selector · Forced \(F_{38}\)) against SimShoot's Two-Stage Selector, which inspects 5 coarse frames and refines the best local interval at zero extra video generation cost—avoiding overshoot and late-frame artifacts.

Interior Frames Chosen: 82.8% (\(F_1\text{--}F_{37}\)) Decisions Changed vs. Terminal-Only: 71.7% (172/240) Single-Round Structure: +2.48 pp Refined Frame Adoption: 67.4% at 0 extra GPU cost
Ablation 2 · Planner (Closed-Loop Replanning)

Adaptive Closed-Loop Replanning vs. Fixed Initial Sequence

What we ablate: Closed-loop feedback in the VLM Planner. Starting from the exact same initial plan, we compare executing that plan open-loop (Fixed Initial Plan) against SimShoot Adaptive Replanning, which inspects each intermediate portrait and selection outcome before deciding the next round. Closed-loop planning yields two key benefits:

  • (1) Dynamic Plan Adaptation: adding emergent refinements or revising the action sequence after observing the realized intermediate portrait.
  • (2) Failure Resilience & Redundancy Avoidance: diagnosing rejected actions to rewrite them with explicit preservation constraints, or stopping early once the goal is already satisfied.

Ablation 3 · Action Simulator (Video vs. Image Modality)

Video Action Simulator vs. Extent-Prompted Image Editors

What we ablate: Why simulate photographic actions with a continuous Video Diffusion Model (VDM) instead of prompting an Image Editing Model at discrete target extents? Holding the Planner and Selector loop fixed, we replace our VDM Action Simulator with FLUX 3 Klein and Qwen-Image 2.1 (prompted at nominal progress \(0\%\text{--}100\%\)), exposing two structural gaps below: non-monotonic single-step progression and multi-round cumulative drift.

Ordered Pairs: 89.7% (0 reversals) vs. 45.6% / 34.9% Strictly Ordered Actions: 31/42 vs. 3/42 & 4/42 Multi-Round Structure Gain: +7.4 to +12.4 pp over image editors

Single-Step Extent Monotonicity (In-Place Synchronized Playback)

All three executors animate in place simultaneously across \(F_0 \to F_{10} \to F_{19} \to F_{29} \to F_{38}\) (\(0\% \to 100\%\) requested extent). Compare smooth continuous progression in SimShoot against non-monotonic jumps, reversal, or scene flicker in independent image edits.

Multi-Round Cumulative Drift & Structural Degradation

Starting from the exact same portrait \(S_0\), step through each executor's states in place (\(S_0 \to S_1 \to S_2 \to \text{Final}\)) via each card's arrows or state pills to directly observe cumulative facial, clothing, and background drift.
